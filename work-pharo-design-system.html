From a fragmented SaaS platform to a unified design system

Location

Rosario, Argentina

Year

2026

Role

Product Designer

Scope

Design system

Building a shared foundation to align visual design, frontend implementation, and AI-assisted prototyping.

A growing platform with increasingly complex needs

Pharo is an enterprise software platform for port management and logistics, comprising multiple specialized modules. As the product evolved, maintaining a cohesive experience across its interfaces became increasingly challenging.

I initiated the development of a design system to bring greater structure to the product’s interface patterns and establish a shared reference for design, development, and AI-assisted prototyping.

The challenge

Scaling a product without a shared design language

Before the design system, interface consistency relied heavily on individual adjustments. Developers created new screens, and I would then refine their interfaces to align them with the intended design.

This reactive process made consistency harder to maintain as the platform grew. Visual properties and interaction patterns could vary between modules, while the lack of centralized documentation made it more difficult to translate design decisions into code consistently.

AI-assisted prototyping added another layer of complexity: without clearly defined components and composition rules, generated interfaces could introduce patterns that diverged from the existing product.

The challenge was to move from screen-by-screen corrections toward a repeatable approach to building and maintaining interfaces.

Solution

Building a structured, reusable system

I developed the design system around Atomic Design, organizing its visual foundations, reusable components, and screen templates into a documented framework.

Foundations

I established the visual foundations, including typography, semantic colors, module-specific accents, and design tokens. Organized in Figma and CSS, these foundations provide a shared reference for visual properties across design and frontend implementation.

Components & templates

Using Atomic Design principles, I structured reusable components and templates for recurring interface patterns and workflows. The components are implemented with Blazor and DevExpress, while the templates provide consistent structures that can be adapted to different screens and modules.

Documentation & AI-assisted workflows

I documented component and template usage, properties, variants, and states. I also created CLAUDE.md to define how Claude should use the system when generating prototypes, including guidelines for component reuse, token usage, and composition.

This documentation connects the Figma library with the implementation and gives AI-assisted workflows a defined set of references to follow.

Impact

From individual adjustments to a shared process

In less than two months, the project evolved from an empty prototyping kit into a structured design system connecting Figma, Blazor implementation, and AI-assisted prototyping.

The most significant change is the way new interfaces can be approached. Rather than relying primarily on visual corrections after development, the team now has documented components, templates, and guidelines to reference when building new screens. As Product Designer, I am responsible for maintaining and evolving this system as the product grows.

The system also establishes a common visual language across modules while preserving their individual identities, and provides Claude with clearer constraints for generating prototypes aligned with the product.

Key learnings

  • A design system needs clear usage rules. Reusable components alone are not enough; documentation helps ensure they are applied consistently.
  • Design and code require continuous alignment. Comparing specifications with implementation helps identify discrepancies and maintain a reliable shared reference.
  • AI-assisted workflows need defined constraints. Documented components, tokens, and composition rules help keep generated interfaces aligned with the system.

The project is ongoing. The next steps are to refine the system, validate its adoption across the platform, and measure its impact on delivery efficiency.
